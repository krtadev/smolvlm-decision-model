Research note

Deciding instead of talking

Phase 1 · SmolVLM-256M-Instruct on ScienceQA · October 2026

This started with Jev, the decision model TypeSafe AI released in September. Jev doesn't chat. You give it some text, a question and the answers it's allowed to give, and it hands back one of those answers with a probability attached. There's no prose to read and nothing to parse, and TypeSafe reports answers in tens to a few hundred milliseconds. Jev works on text. We wanted to know whether the same idea holds up when what you're deciding about is a picture, and whether it survives in a model small enough to run almost anywhere.

The idea is to stop asking the model to write. Ask a normal language model "which colony is shown?" and it answers word by word, each word another full pass through the network, and then something has to read those words and work out which option they meant. A decision model reads the answer off a single spot instead. After one pass over the image, the question and the options, we look only at the scores for the letters A to E at the place where the answer would begin, and turn them into probabilities. Everything the model understands about the picture and the question has to be compressed into that one position, and all of training pushes on that one choice: no capacity goes on phrasing or format. Because the only possible outputs are the options, the answer is always a real option. The probabilities mean something too, so they can be checked against how often the model is right and corrected with a single number. It is also fast for a plain reason: there is no generation loop. The whole input is read in parallel and then we're done, where a written answer pays for one more sequential pass per word. Our 256M version gets through about 50 test questions a second on one GPU, images included.

We trained small LoRA adapters (4.9M parameters) on the language half of SmolVLM-256M, kept the vision encoder frozen, and shuffled the options on every batch so a letter's position meant nothing. Training used the 6,218 ScienceQA questions that come with an image and took thirteen minutes on one L4. Out of the box the model barely decides at all: 39.0% on the test set against 35.5% for guessing, almost the same with the image blanked, and it picks A only 5% of the time although A is right on 36% of questions. After 500 steps it gets 76.5% ± 1.8 on 2,017 questions it had never seen, and the score barely moves however the options are ordered. Blank the images and it drops to 58.5%, so it really is looking. Its confidence is honest: calibration error falls from 0.41 to 0.024, and it averages 82% confidence when it's right and 55% when it's wrong, so you can tell when to trust it. It still struggles with natural science and the rare five-option questions. Validation loss flattened after step 500, which says the next gains will come from a bigger model or more data, not more training.
